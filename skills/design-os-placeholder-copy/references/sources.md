sessions, so fetch them fresh for every task rather than relying on an earlier read. They are
private, so a plain web fetch won't open them. Read them through the designer's own Notion and Google
Drive MCP servers (connectors in Claude chat and Cowork).

### How to use the leadership log

The log explains its own use at the top. For product design work:

- Section 1A (terminology) is binding on every surface.
- Section 1B (product UI principles) governs product screens, modals, and in-product messages.
- Section 4 (brand voice) does not govern product UI. It applies only where copy speaks for the
  company, such as some emails. Section 1B wins any conflict.
- Section 3 (overrides) takes precedence over everything in Section 1.
- Section 2 is evidence, not rules. When the copy resembles a captured scenario (fund bids, review
  emails, rounding tooltips, resale banners), use the matching before/after as precedent.

## Precedence when sources disagree

1. Leadership log Section 3 overrides.
2. On terms, the A to Z wins. On style, the style guide wins. The style guide's own order is: a
   term-specific A to Z rule, then an audience or surface variant, then a style guide section, then
   accuracy over brevity.
3. On legal entity names, structure, and acronyms, the Corporate Cheat Sheet wins over the entity
   rename reference and anything else. Treat every Hiive-era entity name as retired.
4. Where the rebrand naming reference disagrees with the A to Z, the A to Z wins. Two rows in the
   rebrand table are known to be wrong: fund naming is "Clarity Investments funds" with a lowercase
   f, and the product name is "Clarity Plus" with a space.
5. Older entries in the leadership log use "Clarity Fund" and "Clarity Funds." Those predate the
   current fund naming. Apply the log's principle but take the name from the A to Z.

Don't enforce anything the style guide marks with a red flag as unresolved. Those are open
recommendations, not rules. Treat warning-marked items as correct but narrow, and read the note
before applying them. The style guide's "What isn't an error" list is authoritative about what not
to change.

Legal entity and fund names are reproduced exactly as registered. Don't restyle them or expand or
contract their acronyms. Several are near-identical (Clarity Investments, Clarity Investment
Management Inc., Clarity Investment Services Inc., and CIM Funds, LLC are four different things),
so confirm the exact name against the Corporate Cheat Sheet.

If the sources are silent on a term or rule, use judgment consistent with the core rules and flag
it in the handoff as a judgment call. Don't invent a convention silently.

## Known gap

There is no current reference for fund and platform mechanics (structure, eligibility). The A to Z
and the glossary cover terms, not mechanics. When copy depends on how a mechanic works and the
designer hasn't said, ask or flag the assumption.

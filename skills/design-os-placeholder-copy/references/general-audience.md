# General audience mode

Use this mode for every screen not owned by the 3PF, Issuers, or Investing teams, and whenever the
audience is unclear.

## Who the reader is

A wide range of fintech and secondaries experience, including restricted sellers and people with
much less financial knowledge. Some readers have finance backgrounds, but don't count on it. Default
to plain language and more context unless the screen is clearly transactional and the user has
already shown familiarity earlier in the flow.

## How this changes the copy

**Explain rather than compress.** Transactional moments (bidding, listing, offers, money changing
hands) and trust-building moments get more explanation, not less, especially where the user may not
understand the mechanic. Elsewhere, cut words that don't help the user understand or act, but keep
words doing real explanatory work. Unclear brevity is a worse failure than wordiness here.

**Ground unfamiliar terms.** Favor common language over trading jargon. When a term or process is
likely new to the reader, briefly ground it the first time it appears, rather than stating it as if
they already know it.

**Error messages and empty states** don't assume the reader understands the underlying mechanic.
Say what happened and what to do next in terms a newer user can follow.

**One sentence over two**, but don't squeeze two genuinely different ideas into one sentence just to
make it shorter.

**Warm, not marketing.** Copy can be human and encouraging while staying precise and free of
pitching.

## Draft copy from the designer

Keep what works. Copy that is overly terse, or that assumes knowledge this audience likely doesn't
have, counts as not serving the user and is a valid reason to rewrite it even if it's otherwise
clean.

## When to switch modes

If the screen turns out to be for institutional investors, issuers, accredited or qualified
purchasers, or Clarity Investments fund flows, switch to `sophisticated-audience.md` and say so in
the handoff.
